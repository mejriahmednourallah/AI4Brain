AI4BRAIN — Competition POC Specification v0.3
Status: Proposed implementation specificationPrepared: 18 October 2026Phase 1 deadline: 1 November 2026Source: CISTAC 1.0 — AI4BRAIN, Cognitive Growth Agent (2-page challenge brief)
0. Product thesis
AI4BRAIN is a non-clinical, evidence-driven cognitive-growth demonstrator. A learner completes short exercises; an assessment engine creates a provisional performance profile; the system maintains a historical Cognitive Brain Twin; a Knowledge Brain connects exercises, dimensions, and coaching strategies; an AI coach explains the evidence and selects targeted practice; and one data model can be viewed through an Analytics or RPG dashboard.
The distinctive demonstration is not the LLM alone: one observable attempt changes an auditable profile, an explainable, personalized next learning decision, and two visually different dashboards in a consistent way. Adaptive personalization is P0, mandatory for our POC, not a deferred research or polish feature.

3. Exercise specifications
EX-01 — Spatial sequence recall / working memory
Interaction: A 3×3 tile grid lights up in a sequence. User reproduces the order by clicking tiles.
Baseline bank: 6 scored trials: two each at length 3, 4 and 5, shuffled within documented constraints; include 1 unscored practice example.
Item generation: Deterministic seeded sequences with no immediate repeats; same baseline distribution across demo sessions.
Capture: presented_sequence, selected_sequence, item length, item-level order match, per-position order match, response time, interruptions.
Descriptive score: Percentage of correctly reproduced positions across the fixed baseline sequence set. Also show longest_fully_correct_span as a separate observation.
Training adaptation: Gradually increase/decrease sequence length based on repeated full-sequence success; not a global cognitive diagnosis.
Bias / limitation: Motor response, vision, display and familiarity can affect performance. A six-trial span task is a brief sample, not a validated working-memory capacity measurement.
EX-02 — Geometric pattern reasoning / abstract reasoning
Interaction: Choose the missing tile completing a 2×2 or simple 3×3 nonverbal shape pattern from 4 alternatives.
Baseline bank: 6 scored items with explicit rule templates, e.g. shape count, rotation and alternating property; 1 unscored practice example.
Item generation: Deterministic generator: valid rule, one correct answer, three plausible distractors; verify uniqueness of answer.
Capture: rule_type, item version, candidate choices, selected choice, correctness, response time, error category where known.
Descriptive score: 100 × correct_items / 6 (displayed as a preliminary task-set result).
Training adaptation: Increase complexity only after success across different rule templates; rotate templates to reduce memorization.
Bias / limitation: Visual pattern familiarity and language of instructions remain potential confounds. Do not reuse copyrighted commercial matrix-test materials.
EX-03 — Go/no-go / attention and inhibition
Interaction: Tap or press Space for a target color/shape; withhold response for non-target.
Baseline bank: 40 scored trials (30 Go and 10 No-go), randomized with controlled ordering; 6 unscored practice trials.
Timing: Fixed trial timing and response window; save trial stimulus timestamp, response timestamp and possible early presses.
Capture: Hits, omissions, correct rejections, commission errors, median correct-Go reaction time, outliers, focus/visibility interruptions.
Descriptive score: 50 × (hit_rate + correct_rejection_rate) with rates expressed from 0–1. This equal-weights Go and No-go rather than rewarding an always-pressing strategy.
Training adaptation: Gradually vary response-window length or distractor frequency, but never compare those training scores directly with baseline without matching parameters.
Bias / limitation: Browser/device latency, keyboard vs touch, motor ability and display refresh affect timing. Reaction times are secondary information, not a clinical attention diagnosis.
EX-04 — Rule switching / cognitive flexibility
Interaction: Match a target by color or shape based on a clearly displayed current rule. Rules sometimes switch.
Baseline bank: 24 scored trials with about 8 switch trials and 16 repeat trials; practice establishes both rules before scoring.
Item generation: Predeclared balanced switch schedule; avoid ambiguous stimuli that satisfy both rules.
Capture: Rule on each trial, whether it switched, chosen option, correctness, response time, repeat-trial accuracy, switch-trial accuracy.
Descriptive score: 50 × (switch_accuracy + repeat_accuracy) on 0–1 rates; display switch/repeat rates individually.
Training adaptation: Adjust switch frequency or rule complexity after sufficient observations.
Bias / limitation: Only eight switches provide limited precision. Instructions, attention and reading ability also influence performance; interpret the index as performance on this task.
EX-05 — Minimum-move puzzles / planning
Interaction: Solve three small state-transition puzzles by moving tokens under visible constraints. Show the goal but do not provide optimal moves during baseline.
Baseline bank: 3 distinct, quick puzzles with comparable target complexity; show 1 simpler unscored tutorial puzzle.
Item generation: Each puzzle state graph has a known shortest legal path calculated by BFS; all start/goal states are solvable.
Capture: Move sequence, legal/illegal moves, total moves, completion, time, optimal solution length, restarts and undo actions.
Descriptive score: For each completed puzzle, 100 × optimal_moves / actual_legal_moves (capped at 100); incomplete puzzles receive 0 for this simple POC index. Display completion and excess moves separately.
Training adaptation: Recommend puzzles with slightly longer optimal paths or added constraints after repeated efficient completions.
Bias / limitation: Three puzzles are much too few for a stable trait estimate. Familiarity with the interface and puzzle genre strongly matters. Label this result very preliminary.

5. Profile scoring and uncertainty policy
A. What a percentage means
A score is a 0–100 task-performance index, explicitly labeled with its exercise family. It is not a percentile relative to other people, a percentage of intelligence, an IQ estimate, or a clinically validated cognitive trait.
Initial task-specific indices:
Dimension
Initial display index
Working memory
Correct positions / shown positions × 100
Reasoning
Correct patterns / 6 × 100
Attention/inhibition
Mean of Go hit rate and No-go correct-rejection rate × 100
Cognitive flexibility
Mean of repeat-trial and switch-trial accuracy × 100
Planning
Mean of optimal/actual move efficiency for three puzzles, failed = 0
These indices are not cross-dimension equivalent. A 70 in memory does not prove better ability than a 60 in planning. The radar view is a within-task summary visualization, not a general cognitive ranking.
B. Uncertainty, honestly represented
The POC should always display an evidence count and a status such as PRELIMINARY — HIGH UNCERTAINTY, together with the assumption behind that status.
For sets of comparable independent correct/incorrect trials at fixed difficulty (e.g. 6 reasoning items), the prototype may additionally show a simple Beta(1 + successes, 1 + failures) posterior interval. Label it model-based, conditional on exchangeable trials; not psychometric validation. For attention and switching, keep the component rates separate (Go vs No-go; switch vs repeat), and avoid calling a hand-combined interval a validated interval. For planning, three efficiency results do not justify a narrow numeric interval: display insufficient evidence for reliable interval next to the percentage.
If a trial is interrupted, exclude it from scored baseline summaries and flag why. Use fixed baseline parameters for interpretability. Do not infer emotional state from reaction time or mistakes.
C. Historical update rule
Insert raw event in SQLite.
Recompute task-specific summary and uncertainty/evidence indicators using a versioned scorer.
Save a new immutable profile_snapshot with supporting event IDs.
Update the derived Neo4j learner-to-dimension summary relationships.
Tell the coach exactly what changed and why; never allow the LLM to overwrite results.

Knowledge Brain (shared)
Nodes: CognitiveDimension, ExerciseType, ExerciseItem, TrainingStrategy, MisconceptionPattern (optional).Relationships: (ExerciseType)-[:ASSESSES]->(CognitiveDimension), (TrainingStrategy)-[:TARGETS]->(CognitiveDimension), (ExerciseItem)-[:INSTANCE_OF]->(ExerciseType), (TrainingStrategy)-[:ADDRESSES]->(MisconceptionPattern).
Cognitive Brain Twin (per demo user)
Nodes: Learner, DimensionEstimate, ProfileSnapshot, EvidenceSummary, PracticeGoal.Relationships: (Learner)-[:HAS_ESTIMATE]->(DimensionEstimate), (DimensionEstimate)-[:FOR_DIMENSION]->(CognitiveDimension), (ProfileSnapshot)-[:CAPTURES]->(DimensionEstimate), (DimensionEstimate)-[:SUPPORTED_BY]->(EvidenceSummary), (Learner)-[:PREFERS_DASHBOARD]->(DashboardStyle).
For the 2-week POC, these can be two logical subgraphs in one Neo4j instance, with SQLite holding canonical raw events. Populate a small curated graph; no automatic curriculum ingestion.

7.2 Decision policy: assess versus train
Separate missing evidence from observed difficulty. The decision starts with a gate:
If no usable baseline or very little valid evidence exists for a dimension, select an additional assessment before recommending targeted cognitive training for a supposed weakness.
If evidence is usable, choose a training exercise according to a documented priority rule.
Occasionally revisit well-performing or under-sampled dimensions to prevent a permanent focus on one weakness. For the POC, pick the least recently visited dimension every fourth adaptive selection (configurable).
Respect a fixed task availability list, session time budget and the learner's explicit preferences. Do not generate unvalidated assessment items on demand.
Initial training-priority heuristic (not scientifically validated): for dimensions with usable evidence, compute features on [0,1]:
priority = 0.40 × performance_gap + 0.25 × user_goal_weight + 0.20 × practice_recency + 0.15 × uncertainty_need
performance_gap: 1 minus the within-task normalized performance index, only relative to that exercise family. This is a scheduling heuristic, not a comparison of people's general cognitive abilities.
user_goal_weight: declared focus chosen by user; defaults to neutral.
practice_recency: increases if a dimension has not been practiced recently.
uncertainty_need: drives a decision to gather more evidence, not a claim of weak ability. If uncertainty is extreme, prefer an assessment task instead.
Save feature values and selected rule version alongside each recommendation. Coefficients are starting implementation choices; report them as heuristic and tune only against observable demo behavior, not claims of clinical validity.
7.3 Difficulty controller
For training mode only, maintain a recent window of four eligible, comparable, unassisted trials in the same task family and tier:
Evidence window
Next action
At least 3 of 4 successful, no hints
Increase one tier if available
At most 1 of 4 successful
Decrease one tier, or keep the tier and offer a targeted hint if already at minimum
2 of 4 successful, too few trials, or many interruptions
Hold difficulty and gather more evidence
A change requires a traceable difficulty_changed event with old/new tier and supporting attempts. Never apply the same thresholds across different tasks without task-specific success definitions. Baseline assessments remain fixed and uncoached.

7.5 Adaptive plan contract
A TrainingPlan contains: plan_id, profile_snapshot_id, policy_version, goal, created_at, recommended_steps[], estimated_time_minutes, and reason_codes[]. Each step has: dimension_id, exercise_type_id, difficulty_tier, strategy_id (optional), selection_reason, source_evidence_ids, and status.
Generate a short, actionable 2–4-step plan; revise after meaningful new evidence. When an estimate has not changed, say why rather than fabricating progress. The last plan is retained in history so a jury can compare 'before' and 'after'.
7.6 Personalization demonstration scenarios
Scenario A: Reasoning evidence is reasonably complete but performance is low at a specific tier. Select an intermediate reasoning exercise and a rule-comparison strategy.
Scenario B: Planning result is low but based on only three puzzles. Prioritize another planning assessment instead of asserting weak planning ability.
Scenario C: Four eligible training trials have high success without hints. Raise the relevant task tier; show the exact rule, inputs and previous tier.
Scenario D: User switches Analytics to RPG. All cognitive data and recommendations remain unchanged; only their presentation differs.
7.7 Lightweight implementation and guardrails
Use plain Python profile_service.py, selector.py, difficulty.py, strategy.py, and plan_service.py behind FastAPI. Store policy configuration as a versioned JSON/Python constant; persist event and recommendation history to SQLite; mirror only useful relationships to Neo4j. No reinforcement learning, no training pipeline, no personalization microservices.
Minimum smoke checks (not a formal testing workstream): deterministic re-score of one saved attempt set; insufficient-evidence case; four-trial difficulty promotion/demotion; two-profile divergent recommendation; dashboard mode invariance; recovery when the LLM is unavailable. These are essential to a reliable live demo.
8. Coach, collaboration score, and dashboard
Coach
Input is a controlled summary: latest evidence, uncertainty, error history, relevant exercises/strategies from the Knowledge Brain, the personalization engine's selected intervention and reason codes, and valid next actions. Output JSON contains {observation, explanation, recommended_exercise_id, recommended_difficulty, rationale, caveat}. The server checks recommendation IDs/difficulty ranges; the coach may propose alternatives, but cannot change task scores, verified evidence, or adaptation decisions directly. Text-only coaching must work independently of voice features.
Natural collaboration score
Capture opportunities for clarification (20%), critical verification (25%), iteration (20%), autonomy (20%), responsible use (15%). Score a dimension only when there has been a genuine opportunity to exhibit the behavior. Initially provide not enough collaboration evidence if there are too few events. Once enough exists, calculate a 0–100 weighted score with transparent event references; never equate high message count with good collaboration. The brief's weights are explicitly a proposal, not validated.
Dashboards
Analytics: radar of five preliminary indices; historical progress line chart; heatmap of completed activities; goal; uncertainty/evidence drill-down; collaboration score when available.
RPG: same data, shown through quests, XP for completing practice, levels and a skill tree. XP is not a cognitive score. A small link shows the underlying evidence and uncertainty.
Switch between modes without recalculating estimates. No real peer percentile/leaderboard without a justified comparison sample.

12. Risks and scientific claims we will not make
Five short exercises cannot establish a validated cognitive/personality profile. The first profile is a set of provisional task-performance indicators.
The five percentages are not inherently comparable across tasks. Our dashboard must disclose this and avoid an overall 'intelligence score'.
Confidence intervals can be misleading with adaptive trials, tiny samples and unknown item equivalence. Use fixed baseline items and visibly label assumptions; prefer 'insufficient evidence' when appropriate.
Training improvements are not proof of far transfer into school performance, work performance, or general intelligence.
Browser reaction times are affected by hardware/software. Record conditions, interpret cautiously.
Personality labels inferred from short interactions are weak. Dashboards represent user preferences, not fixed psychological types.
No AI-generated answer or coach judgment may directly change validated-by-code scores.
Demo history must be labeled sample/seeded where it is not collected from actual sessions.
